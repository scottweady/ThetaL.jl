Kinematics and the rigid split

A curve in θ–L variables

A closed counterclockwise curve $\Gamma$ of length $L$ is parametrized by $\alpha \in [0, 2\pi)$ at equal arclength, $s_\alpha = L/2\pi$. Its tangent angle is $\theta(\alpha) = \alpha + \phi(\alpha)$ with $\phi$ periodic, and the curve is

\[x(\alpha) = x_0 + \frac{L}{2\pi} \int_0^\alpha (\cos\theta, \sin\theta) \, d\alpha'.\]

The state of a curve (ThetaLState) is $\phi$ at $N$ equispaced $\alpha$, the length $L$ and the point $x_0 = x(0)$. The tangent is $\tau = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$, the outward normal $n = (\sin\theta, -\cos\theta)$, and the curvature $\kappa = \theta_s = (1 + \phi_\alpha)/s_\alpha$, positive on convex parts. A state is made from any parametrization by from_curve (spectrally, with N chosen to a tolerance) or from points already at equal arclength by from_arclength_samples.

The motion

The points move with $x_t = U n + T \tau$: $U$ is the given normal velocity, and the tangential velocity $T$ is chosen to keep the points at equal arclength (Hou, Lowengrub & Shelley 1994):

\[L_t = \int_0^{2\pi} U \, \theta_\alpha \, d\alpha,\]

\[T_\alpha = \frac{L_t}{2\pi} - U \theta_\alpha, \qquad T(0) = 0,\]

\[\theta_t = \frac{T \theta_\alpha - U_\alpha}{s_\alpha}, \qquad x_{0,t} = U(0)\, n(0).\]

thetaL_rhs(X, U, filt) computes $\theta_t$, $L_t$ and $x_{0,t}$.

The rigid split

With $T(0) = 0$ the points slide along a curve that turns, at a rate set by the tangential velocity at $x_0$, which changes as $x_0$ slides; and $x_0$ itself moves on a circle that an explicit step cannot follow exactly. So no time integrator of these equations keeps a rigid rotation exact. ThetaL splits the normal velocity of each curve, at every stage of every step, into its rigid part and the rest:

\[U = U_{\mathrm{rig}} + U_d, \qquad U_{\mathrm{rig}} = V \cdot n - \Omega\, (x - x_c) \cdot \tau,\]

where the translation $V$ and rotation rate $\Omega$ about the center $x_c$ (the mean of the grid points) give the rigid normal velocity closest to $U$ on the grid (least squares; rigid_part). The rigid part moves the curve without moving the points along it: it adds $\Omega$ to $\theta_t$ and carries $x_0$ on the rigid motion, $z' = i\Omega z + a$ in complex form, which the step integrates exactly. The deformation $U_d$ goes through the equations above. Under a rigid motion $\hat\phi_k$ ($k \neq 0$) and $L$ stay fixed and only the mean of $\phi$ moves, by $\Omega h$, which every scheme integrates exactly: translation and rotation are exact to roundoff at any time step (Method overview, panel (a)).

The split needs only $U$, not the material tangential velocity. A curve whose shape is steady while material flows along it (an edge current) has $U = 0$ and stays exactly steady. On a circle the rotation is invisible in $U$ and is left out (rigid_part drops it when $(x - x_c) \cdot \tau$ vanishes to roundoff). CurveProblem(...; rigid = :translation) takes out the translation only; rigid = :none gives the plain Hou–Lowengrub–Shelley step.

The velocity of the tracked points

A bulk mesh that follows the grid points needs their velocity, not only its normal part: frame_velocity(X, U) returns $U n + T\tau$ with the tangential velocity of the step (the rigid motion's, plus the equal-arclength one of the deformation).
